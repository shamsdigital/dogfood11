# Carnicería 2 Toros — Page-by-Page Website Copy Review

**Production domain:** `dostorosmeatmarket.com`
**Reviewed preview:** `toros-4op.pages.dev`
**Copy objective:** Make the two stores, their family story, local role, and practical visitor information easy to understand. Product listings should support visit/order decisions, not become the brand's main story.

> **Important:** Drafts below are ready for editorial use, but facts marked **[VERIFY]** must be checked by the business before publishing. In particular, confirm which phone number, services, and ordering options apply to each store; confirm current promotions and the exact street-address formatting. Keep only claims that are true today.

## Whole-site voice and consistency rules

Use **Carnicería 2 Toros Meat Market** as the full business name on first mention, then **2 Toros** naturally. Choose one accent/spelling convention for headings and metadata, but make sure the visible brand name matches how the business presents itself in the real world and on its Google Business Profiles. The current site uses several forms—“Carniceria,” “Carnicería,” “Tienda 2 Toros,” and “2 Toros”—so settle on a primary name and use alternate names only where useful.

Prefer specific, human wording over generic sales claims. “Serving Danville since 2015” is useful if accurate; “the neighborhood's most beloved market” is hard to substantiate and should be removed unless there is a defensible basis. Avoid “freshest,” “best,” or “highest-quality” as unprovable superlatives. Explain concrete differences instead: house-made tortillas, named weekend traditions, or services offered in-store.

Use the two-location distinction consistently. A visitor should never have to infer which store an address, menu, offer, service, phone, or set of hours refers to. Repeat accurate hours and location details on the relevant page, then make them easy to act on with separate “Directions,” “Call,” and “Text/WhatsApp” links.

Do not publish artificial city-swapped paragraphs. Each location page needs actual location-specific facts, photos, directions, and useful visit details. Do not write product-page copy for search volume when the desired outcome is discovery of the stores and their community role.

## Final publication checklist

Before approving each page, ask:

1. Is every fact true right now, and does this page clearly say which store it applies to?

1. Can a first-time visitor find the address, hours, directions, and right contact method without hunting?

1. Does the text say what is distinctive about the family, store, tradition, service, or local involvement—or is it interchangeable marketing copy?

1. Are offers, menu items, prices, provider names, availability, and time-based statements current?

1. Are first-person claims, founder details, customer quotes, and photos approved by the people involved?

1. Does the page help a visitor decide to visit, contact, or understand the community role, without turning the site into a product-promotion catalog?
